What I understand better about algorithms now:
 
I now understand that algorithms are not just raw code, but the structured thinking that must happen before coding begins. Breaking a problem down using sequence, decisions, and repetition makes the logic clear to anyone reading it. I also learned that handling edge cases explicitly in plain text prevents major bugs later. Writing strong pseudocode forces me to be completely honest about whether a solution actually works.
How tracing changed my confidence: 
Tracing manually with sample inputs proved to me exactly how a computer will execute my logic. It removed the guesswork from my process because I can physically track variables changing step-by-step. Instead of just hoping the algorithm works, I now have the confidence to prove it works before I even open a code editor.
